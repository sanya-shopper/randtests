TestU01 (L'Ecuyer & Simard, 2007)

The gold standard. TestU01 collected essentially every empirical test in the literature into one ANSI C library with adjustable parameters, then bolted three predefined batteries on top. Since 2007, "passes BigCrush" has been the sentence a new generator must be able to say [L'Ecuyer & Simard 2007].

History and design

Pierre L'Ecuyer spent the 1980s and 90s building the theory of generator quality — spectral figures of merit, equidistribution, combined generators. TestU01, written with Richard Simard and published in ACM TOMS in 2007, is that program made executable. Its stated motivations read as a critique of the field: Diehard's samples were too small and its parameters frozen; NIST's battery was too weak; nobody could compare results across ad-hoc implementations. TestU01 wraps any generator as a C object (unif01_Gen), offers the tests with parameters exposed, and defines standard batteries so that "we ran Crush" means the same thing everywhere [L'Ecuyer & Simard 2007].

The test population spans this whole site: serial and collision tests, gap, poker, coupon collector, max-of-t, birthday spacings in high dimensions, close pairs, matrix rank, Hamming-weight dependence, random walks, linear complexity, Lempel–Ziv, Fourier — the classical catalog, the Diehard inventions, and the modern additions, each generalised and parameterised.

What it found

The TOMS paper's famous results table ran dozens of generators in wide use — language defaults, textbook recommendations, Diehard survivors — through the batteries. Most failed. The Mersenne Twister's two BigCrush failures (MatrixRank and LinearComp, the GF(2)-linearity signature) are the best-known line, but the table's larger message was that passing the previous era's tests meant little: the ratchet of stringency had turned again [L'Ecuyer & Simard 2007] [Vigna 2019]. Every serious generator proposal since — PCG, xoshiro/xoroshiro, counter-based generators — reports BigCrush results as a matter of course [Salmon et al. 2011].

Strengths and blind spots

Strengths. Breadth (the whole literature in one library), parameterisation, standardisation, and the theoretical pedigree to choose test parameters where power actually lies. Rabbit/Alphabit extend coverage to finite files from hardware sources.

Blind spots. TestU01 interprets output as [0,1) doubles built from 32-bit values, so it weights high-order bits: a generator with weak low bits can pass until the stream is bit-reversed, and 64-bit generators must be fed as split or reversed halves — testing conventions the user must know. Batteries are fixed-size rather than sequential: they answer "did 2³⁸ values fail?" but not "how far must I go before it fails?", which is PractRand's question. The library is single-threaded C with a learning curve, last released in 2009 and now community-mirrored. And like everything on this site, it certifies nothing cryptographic: AES-CTR passes BigCrush with any key; that is a fact about the tests, not about key security.
