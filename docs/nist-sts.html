NIST SP 800-22 (the Statistical Test Suite)

The battery with a badge. Published in 2001 and revised through 2010, NIST's Statistical Test Suite grew out of the AES competition's need to check candidate ciphers' output streams, and became the test suite that certification regimes name explicitly — which makes both its contents and its documented flaws consequential [NIST SP 800-22].

The fifteen tests

All operate on a bit sequence of length n (10⁶ bits is the customary configuration). Nearly every one funnels into the chi-square/igamc machinery or the complementary error function.

The two-level evaluation

SP 800-22 institutionalises the two-level scheme. Test m sequences (say 1000 × 10⁶ bits) at α = 0.01; then, per test [NIST SP 800-22]:

proportion check: pass rate within (1−α) ± 3·√(α(1−α)/m) — for m = 1000, roughly 0.981 to 0.999
uniformity check: bin the m p-values into 10 cells, χ² with 9 df, require P-valueT = igamc(9/2, χ²/2) ≥ 0.0001

Published criticisms

Being the compliance battery has drawn two decades of scrutiny, much of it substantiated. The DFT test's original reference distribution was simply wrong — corrected constants were derived by Kim, Umeno and Hasegawa and the test has been re-analysed repeatedly since [Kim et al. 2004]; the related Lempel–Ziv test was dropped in the 2008 revision. The fifteen tests are far from independent — approximate entropy overlaps serial, cusum overlaps frequency — so "passed 15 of 15" overstates the evidence [Appl. Math. Comput. 2023]. The second-level procedure itself has documented inaccuracies: Pareschi, Rovatti and Setti showed the pass-proportion approximation misbehaves at standard settings [Pareschi et al. 2012], and stronger second-level procedures were proposed at ASIACRYPT 2016 [Zhu et al. 2016]. Above all, the suite's discriminating power is modest: generators that sail through SP 800-22 fail Crush or PractRand within minutes [L'Ecuyer & Simard 2007].

NIST opened a formal revision in 2022 through its Crypto Publication Review Project; the direction of travel, argued in the public comments, is to decouple the battery from cryptographic-security claims — statistical testing as sanity check, cryptanalysis as the real bar [NIST review 2022]. The document itself has always said as much: statistical testing "cannot serve as a substitute for cryptanalysis" [NIST SP 800-22].

For entropy sources (as opposed to deterministic generators), NIST uses a different instrument entirely — SP 800-90B's entropy estimation and continuous health tests, covered on the other suites page.

Where it fits

Strengths. A freely available, documented, standard battery with reference code; the two-level methodology is spelled out precisely; it remains the shared vocabulary of certification labs, and its tests (rank, linear complexity, Maurer universal, excursions) are solid ideas drawn from real cryptanalytic concerns.

Weaknesses. Known statistical errors, correlated tests, a fragile second level, and power far below the modern suites — a 2001 instrument still doing a 2026 job while its revision is pending.
